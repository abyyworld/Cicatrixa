What it does, in order

  1. 01DetectTails container logs and polls health. Three errors in a thirty-second window, or three failed health checks, trips it.
  2. 02DiagnoseTraceback plus an index of the source goes to a reasoning model, which returns a structured root cause — file, exact line, and an input that triggers it.
  3. 03ReproduceWrites a test and runs it in a throwaway container to confirm it fails on the current code. No reproduction, no patch — the pipeline stops here rather than guessing.
  4. 04FixIterates a patch until the reproduction passes and the existing suite stays green. Both checked in ephemeral containers, never on the live service.
  5. 05ShipBuilds the patched image and moves 20% of traffic to it. Watches error rate for the window, then promotes to 100% or rolls back. The old version stays at weight zero as an instant undo.

What we will not claim

A passing test suite is not proof that a fix works. If no test executes the lines a patch changed, a green suite only shows the patch broke nothing that was already covered — which is exactly what a confidently wrong patch looks like.

So every fix is recorded at the level its evidence supports, and no higher. Coverage is intersected against the specific lines changed. The level is computed from the run, never asserted by the thing that wants it to be high.

unverified_no_coverage — the default, and a legitimate place to stop
verified_existing_tests — suite green and your tests execute the changed lines
verified_reproduction — a test that failed before the patch and passes after
verified_merged_stable — merged, and stable in production since

If we could not prove it, we say so. That is the whole product.
